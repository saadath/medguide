Generalized Pruritus

Structured clinical workup — from first-line labs to a full diagnostic algorithm

Clinical Guide
Introduction

Approach to Generalized Pruritus

GOAL 01

Evaluate Allergic & Skin Causes

Assess for primary dermatologic disease and investigate allergic etiologies when the history supports them.

GOAL 02

Exclude Systemic Causes

Systematically rule out systemic, metabolic, hematologic, and infectious etiologies of generalized pruritus.

Key concept: Generalized pruritus is not necessarily allergic in origin. When no primary skin lesion is present — Pruritus sine materia — systemic causes must be taken far more seriously.

Step 2 — Targeted Testing

Allergic Workup

If the history points toward allergy, order tests in a targeted way — guided by the clinical picture, not as a blanket panel.

TestIndication
Total IgE
Screening		When atopic disease is suspected — not a specific screening test
Specific IgE
Allergen-Specific		Sensitivity to a defined allergen — foods, dust, mites, pollen, animals
Skin Prick Test
In-Vivo · Immediate		Immediate (IgE-mediated) allergy
Patch Test
In-Vivo · Delayed		Allergic contact dermatitis
Serum Tryptase
Mast Cell		Suspected mastocytosis or recurrent anaphylactic reactions
Requesting a broad Food Allergy panel — or Total IgE alone — is not recommended for identifying the cause of generalized pruritus.
A positive Specific IgE does not necessarily indicate clinical allergy — results must always be interpreted alongside the history.

Red flags: In chronic unexplained pruritus — especially with weight loss, night sweats, fever, or lymphadenopathy — proceed to hematologic workup and imaging such as CXR and abdominal ultrasound.

Allergy Workup — Targeted, Only if History Supports

  • Total IgE — suspected atopic disease; not a specific screening test
  • Specific IgE — defined allergen: foods, dust, mites, pollen, animals
  • Skin Prick Test — immediate (IgE-mediated) allergy
  • Patch Test — allergic contact dermatitis
