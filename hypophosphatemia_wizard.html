Emergency Treatment Protocol — Initiate Immediately
Severe or symptomatic hypophosphatemia requires inpatient IV phosphorus replacement and close monitoring.
  1. Hospital admission — ICU/step-down unit if hemodynamic instability, respiratory failure, or serious arrhythmia is present.
  2. IV phosphorus replacement
    • Use sodium phosphate if serum K+ ≥ 4.0 mEq/L; potassium phosphate if hypokalemic.
    • Dose: 0.08 – 0.24 mmol/kg elemental phosphorus (≈ 2.5 – 7.5 mg/kg) IV over 4 – 6 hours.
    • Maximum single dose: ≈ 30 mmol.
    • Recheck serum phosphorus 4 – 6 h after infusion ends before redosing.
  3. Continuous monitoring
    • Continuous ECG / telemetry (QT interval, arrhythmias).
    • Serum phosphorus, calcium, potassium, magnesium q6 – 12 h during replacement.
    • Pulse oximetry ± ABG; ventilatory support if respiratory failure.
  4. Cautions
    • Avoid aggressive over-correction — risk of hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia, tetany, and arrhythmia.
    • Correct concurrent hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia (refractory without correction).
    • Reduce dose in renal impairment; monitor for volume overload.
  5. Address the underlying cause in parallel (DKA, refeeding syndrome, sepsis, burns, diuretic use, parathyroid disorders).

Etiology — Renal vs Non-Renal

Determine the mechanism of hypophosphatemia using the Fractional Excretion of Phosphate (FEPO4) from concurrent serum and spot urine samples.

FEPO4 Formula
FEPO4 (%) = (U-PO4 × S-Cr) / (S-PO4 × U-Cr) × 100
Interpretation in hypophosphatemia: < 5% → non-renal cause (appropriate renal conservation)  ·  ≥ 5% → renal phosphate wasting.
