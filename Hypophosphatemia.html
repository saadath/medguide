Emergency Management Recommended

The patient meets criteria for severe or critical hypophosphatemia. Immediate hospital-based management is indicated.

  • Admit the patient to hospital (consider ICU / telemetry).
  • Obtain a 12-lead ECG and initiate continuous cardiac monitoring.
  • Start intravenous phosphorus replacement (e.g., sodium phosphate) per local protocol.
  • Monitor serum phosphorus, calcium, magnesium and potassium serially.
  • Watch closely for arrhythmias, respiratory muscle weakness, rhabdomyolysis and hemolysis.
  • Treat the underlying cause; consider critical care / nephrology consultation.
  • Caution: rapid IV phosphorus can cause hypocalcemia, hypotension and metastatic calcification — infuse slowly and recheck levels.

Etiology — Renal vs Non‑Renal

The key discriminator is urinary phosphate excretion. Fill in the four values below — as soon as all are entered, FePO₄ and TmP/GFR are calculated live, the etiology branch is determined, and the matching differential diagnosis appears.

Phosphate Excretion Calculator

FePO₄ is calculated from a fasting serum phosphate with a simultaneous spot urine phosphate and creatinine. TmP/GFR is derived from the same samples and is considered the reference method.

Enter all four values to calculate FePO₄ and TmP/GFR and determine the etiology branch.

Cutoff: FePO₄ ≥ 5 % (or a low TmP/GFR) indicates renal phosphate wasting. FePO₄ < 5 % indicates a non‑renal cause.
